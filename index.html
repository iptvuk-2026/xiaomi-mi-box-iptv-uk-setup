Setting Up IPTV on Xiaomi Mi Box S and Realme 4K Stick in the UK

Two of the cheapest ways to put IPTV on a television are the Xiaomi Mi Box S and the Realme 4K Smart Google TV Stick — both far cheaper than a premium box, both 4K, both running an IPTV player from the Play Store.

Two Budget Devices, One Job

Not every television is a smart TV, and not every smart TV runs the apps its owner wants. A streaming box or stick fixes that for the price of a takeaway or two. The Mi Box S is a small box beside the television; the Realme stick hides behind it, plugged into an HDMI socket.

Both share the same strengths and limits: 2 GB of memory and 8 GB of storage each, 4K at 60 frames per second, a voice remote and Chromecast built in — and no Ethernet port, so both rely on Wi-Fi. Both run IPTV through a player app, logging in with the same Xtream Codes or M3U playlist details that work on a Firestick.

One wrinkle: Xiaomi has sold several generations under the Mi Box S name. The original runs Android TV with a Cortex-A53 processor, 2 GB of RAM and 8 GB of storage; later ones run Google TV, add Dolby Vision and HDR10+, and the newest ships with 32 GB. Steps here work on all of them; quoted specifications mean the original unless stated.

Both are Google-certified, so the Play Store works and protected video plays at full resolution — unlike uncertified boxes, stuck at standard definition.

The real differences are physical: the box gives you a USB port for a keyboard or a wired network adapter; the stick gives you only a power socket.

Which One Suits Your Living Room?

Pick the Mi Box S if…

Your television sits on a stand with room beside it, you want a USB keyboard for typing playlist addresses, or you may later add a wired network adapter.

Pick the Realme stick if…

Your television is wall-mounted and you want nothing visible, you travel and want a pocket-sized streamer, or you prefer the Google TV home screen, which gathers recommendations from your apps.

Whichever you choose, the IPTV experience is the same: the player, the channel list and the picture quality are decided by your subscription and broadband, not the device.

Before You Switch Anything On

Ten minutes of preparation saves half an hour behind the television.

  • A Google account for sign-in and the Play Store. Have your phone nearby if you use two-step verification.
  • Your Wi-Fi password. Both are Wi-Fi only; use the 5 GHz band for 4K if the router is in the same room.
  • A spare HDMI port and a mains socket. The stick can run from a television USB port, but the mains adapter is more reliable.
  • Your IPTV login details from iptvuk.store: a server address, username and password for Xtream Codes, or an M3U playlist address.
  • Broadband fast enough for 4K: about 25 Mbps for 4K, 8 to 12 Mbps for high definition, 3 to 5 Mbps for standard definition. See the internet speed guide if unsure.

Accept the system update offered during setup: an out-of-date box is the most common cause of apps refusing to install.

Setting Up the Xiaomi Mi Box S

Allow about fifteen minutes, including the update.

  1. Connect it up. Plug HDMI into the box and the television, connect the power adapter, and switch the television to the right input.
  2. Pair the remote. Hold it close to the box and follow the prompt; if it fails, hold home and back together and retry.
  3. Pick a language and region. Choose English (UK) and the United Kingdom.
  4. Connect to Wi-Fi. Choose your network, enter the password, and pick the 5 GHz band if it appears.
  5. Sign in with Google. Use a phone to complete sign-in when offered — far faster than typing with a remote.
  6. Accept the system update. Let it download, install and restart the box. Do not unplug it mid-update.
  7. Set the display. In Settings, then Display, choose 4K at 60 Hz, HDR on automatic.
  8. Open the Play Store and install your IPTV player. TiviMate, IPTV Smarters Pro and XCIPTV are listed for Android TV; if yours is missing, try another from the apps section.

If the box feels sluggish, its 8 GB is filling with pre-installed apps — uninstall ones you will never open.

Setting Up the Realme 4K Stick

The stick runs Google TV, so the screens look different, but the steps match.

  1. Plug the stick into HDMI. Use the supplied extender if it will not fit, and power it from the mains adapter.
  2. Switch to the right input and wait for the Google TV welcome screen. If nothing appears, try another HDMI socket.
  3. Set up with the Google Home app. Let it set itself up from your phone — it copies your Wi-Fi password and Google account across in seconds.
  4. Choose English (UK) and the United Kingdom as your region.
  5. Let it update. Google TV usually downloads an update on first setup; let it finish and restart first.
  6. Check the picture settings. In Settings, then Display and Sound, confirm 4K at 60 Hz, HDR on automatic.
  7. Install your IPTV player from the Play Store on the home screen. The same players work here, with identical login details.
  8. Pin the player to your favourites. Google TV buries apps in the Apps tab; pinning keeps yours one press away.

One warning: only about 4 GB of its 8 GB is free for apps once the system takes its share, so install your player first and remove apps you stop using.

Installing Your IPTV Player

The device is only a screen; the player app turns your subscription into channels. Install from the Play Store first: a store install updates itself and is checked by Google. Search by name and check the developer matches the one on the TiviMate, IPTV Smarters Pro or XCIPTV pages.

If your player is not listed, sideloading is the fallback: install Downloader from the Play Store, allow it to install unknown apps, then fetch the player from its official site. Never download a player from a random search result.

Keep player apps to one: each keeps its own copy of the channel list and guide, and on 8 GB that fills the device fast.

Logging In: Xtream Codes or M3U

Every player asks for the same details in different words. Xtream Codes is easier: open the player, choose Login with Xtream Codes API, and enter the server address, username and password from your iptvuk.store subscription. The player downloads the channel list, guide and on-demand catalogue itself; a first sync can take several minutes.

M3U works the same way with one field: choose Login with M3U URL, paste the playlist address, and name it IPTV UK. Some players also ask for a guide address, found in the same welcome message. A mistyped character is the most common login failure, so copy and paste the address from your phone. If it fails, check the address, then your Wi-Fi — see the login problems guide.

Once in, hide channel groups you will never watch, mark regulars as favourites, and check the guide has loaded for tomorrow too.

Picture and Sound Settings That Matter

Three settings decide how either device looks. Match the frame rate to the content if your player offers it: a 50 Hz broadcast at 60 Hz judders, often blamed on the stream when it is really the box. Leave HDR on automatic, so standard-definition channels are not forced through HDR. If your soundbar supports pass-through audio, enable it; if you hear silence, switch back to stereo.

Wi-Fi placement matters more than any setting. Both are Wi-Fi only, so put the router in the same room if you can and use 5 GHz for 4K. If the router is two rooms away, a Mi Box S can take a wired network adapter through its USB port; a Realme stick cannot, so use a booster or powerline adapter. Keep 1 GB free, clearing a cache in Settings, then Apps, when a player stutters, as the buffering fixes describe.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I watch IPTV in 4K on a Mi Box S or a Realme stick?

Yes, if your subscription includes 4K streams, your television is 4K, and your broadband delivers about 25 Mbps steadily. Both decode 4K at 60 frames per second — the stream, screen and connection must all be 4K together.

Which is better for IPTV, the Mi Box S or the Realme stick?

For IPTV itself, neither: the player and the subscription decide the experience, and both run the same players. Choose on shape and ports — the box for USB and easy access, the stick to hide behind a wall-mounted television.

Do I need to sideload apps on either device?

Usually not: the mainstream players are listed in the Play Store for both devices. Sideload only if yours is not listed for your model, and only from the player’s own site.

Why does my device say storage is full with only a few apps installed?

Both have 8 GB, and the system takes roughly half before you install anything; each player also stores its own channel list and guide. Uninstall unused apps and clear caches regularly.

Can I use the same subscription on both devices?

That depends on your plan: some allow one screen at a time, others several. Check the pricing page and your plan’s connection limit before logging in on a second device.

The Verdict: Budget Devices, Proper Setup

The Mi Box S and the Realme stick prove IPTV does not need an expensive box: either puts live channels, on-demand films and a programme guide on any television with HDMI. The trade-off is small — 8 GB of storage to manage and Wi-Fi instead of a cable.

Set either up as above, install one good player, log in with your IPTV UK details from iptvuk.store, and keep a gigabyte free. If you are still choosing a plan, see the pricing page.
